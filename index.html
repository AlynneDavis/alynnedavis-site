Does Any of This Sound Familiar?

Maybe you love being a mom, and still you wonder if there has to be more than this.

Maybe you’re lonely in a life that looks full from the outside.

Maybe you’ve been made small for so long that wanting more now feels almost dangerous.

  • Whether it’s motherhood, divorce, perimenopause, or a relationship you’ve lost yourself in, you no longer recognize the woman in the mirror.
  • Food became comfort, and your body became something to fight instead of live in.
  • No matter how much you do, you still feel like you’re not enough.
  • You show up for everyone, keep the peace, and anticipate what people need before they have to ask, until you stopped asking what you needed at all.
  • You’ve spent so long trying not to disappoint anyone that you can barely hear your own voice anymore.
  • You’ve hoped for real emotional connection in your marriage and been dismissed, shut out, or shamed instead.
  • You’ve been walking on eggshells so long you’ve forgotten what it feels like to relax in your own home.
  • You’ve tried therapy before and still can’t figure out why you can’t get organized or feel like yourself.
  • You’re so exhausted you can barely cope, and some days you feel trapped.
  • You want healing that goes deeper than just coping strategies.

You may have spent years believing the problem was you.

Nothing is wrong with you.

These are the ways you learned to survive. They worked, until they stopped.

A Gentler Way Back to Yourself

My work blends somatic and nervous system practices, creative expression, and deep, compassionate conversation to help women reconnect with themselves, especially in seasons when life has changed and you no longer recognize your own reflection.

That means the work happens in your body, not only in your head. And you do not need to be artistic. Creative expression here simply means finding ways to reach what words alone cannot.

My approach is also holistic, which means I look at your whole life rather than a diagnosis. Sleep, nourishment, movement, relationships, spirituality, self-care, and the environment you live in all shape how you feel, and none of that shows up on an intake form. We pay attention to all of it, because you are a whole person, not a set of symptoms.

Many of the women I work with have spent years pushing through, holding it together, or trying to make themselves "better" through willpower alone. The way back usually begins somewhere much gentler: slowing down, and becoming curious about the patterns shaping your life.

Whether we work together in therapy, an intensive, or coaching, the aim is the same: a place where you can understand yourself with more compassion and clarity, and begin to feel like you again.

This process is not about fixing what is broken. It is about reconnecting with the parts of you that have been carrying too much, for too long.

Frequently Asked Questions

What’s the difference between therapy, intensives, and coaching?

Therapy is ongoing, licensed mental health care for women in North Carolina and South Carolina. Intensives are focused, extended sessions for going deep more quickly. Coaching, through the Embodied Reclamation Studio, is forward-focused personal growth work available to women anywhere, and is not therapy. Not sure which fits? That’s exactly what a free consultation is for.

How do I know if therapy is right for me?

If you feel stuck in patterns that are painful or confusing and want support in understanding yourself more deeply, therapy can help.

What does expressive arts therapy involve?

Expressive arts therapy integrates creative practices such as drawing, writing, movement, and reflection to help access emotions and insights that may be difficult to express through words alone.

Do you offer therapy in Charlotte, North Carolina?

Yes. I provide trauma-informed expressive arts therapy for women in Charlotte and surrounding communities.

Do I need to be artistic to benefit from expressive arts therapy?

Not at all. Expressive arts therapy is not about artistic skill. It is about exploration, creativity, and connecting with your inner experience in new ways.

Who do you work with?

I work with women in transition, navigating divorce, motherhood, midlife, loss, a new diagnosis, or simply the sense of having lost themselves along the way. Many are also healing eating disorders, trauma, ADHD, body image struggles, perfectionism, and patterns of coping or control.

What Happens After You Reach Out

  1. You send a short message. Just your name, your email, and a little about what you are looking for. It does not need to be polished or complete.
  2. You get an email with my number in it. If reaching out directly feels easier than waiting, you can.
  3. Then you hear from me. I follow up as soon as I can, usually by text, and we find a time that works around a schedule that probably already feels full.
  4. We talk for about 15 minutes. A chance to share what is going on and ask anything you want to know. There is no pressure to commit, and no obligation to continue.
