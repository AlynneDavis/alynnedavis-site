The Embodied Self-Discovery Path

For women who have lost connection to themselves

You have spent years being the one who holds it all together. For your kids, your parents, a partner, a family that needed you to be steady. Maybe it was a relationship where your feelings were too much, so you learned to have fewer of them. Maybe it was a divorce, a loss, an emptier house, or a body that changed, and one day you looked up and did not know who you are when no one needs anything from you.

Nothing about that means you are broken. You adapted. The ways you learned to leave yourself made sense at the time, and they worked, until they started costing more than they gave. Trauma is often woven into how that happened, and this work makes room for it, gently and at your pace.

The Embodied Self-Discovery path brings together psychological exploration, the MARI® assessment, parts work, somatic awareness, expressive arts, and guided reflection. It happens in two stages.

Begin With The Foundation

Every Self-Discovery path begins with The Foundation: two sessions that help us understand who you became, and why it made sense.

  • An initial assessment and MARI® experience of about 90 minutes, exploring your history and what brings you here
  • My personal interpretation of your MARI®, about two hours I spend between sessions studying what emerged, so our review begins from a considered picture of you
  • A two-hour review of what emerged and the parts of you that showed up

The Foundation can stand on its own. Some women take what they learn into their lives or into ongoing therapy. Many choose to continue.

Then, the Self-Discovery Series

The Series is three more two-hour sessions that build on what The Foundation revealed. This is where understanding becomes something you live.

  • Two sessions of experiential work with the parts and patterns that surfaced, through expressive arts, somatic awareness, and guided reflection
  • A personalized integration session, including a plan for what comes next, whether that is ongoing therapy, coaching, or carrying the work forward on your own
  • A companion self-discovery journal to support the process between sessions and beyond

The Series follows The Foundation and is best begun while what you discovered is still fresh. If you continue within 30 days, the Series is offered at a reduced rate.

What Embodied Reclamation Looks Like

Together, The Foundation and the Series follow the RRR Method™. The Foundation leans into Remember: understanding who you became. The Series leans into Reclaim: choosing what you carry forward. And you are returning to yourself the whole way through, in your body as much as your mind.

The goal isn’t to become someone different. It’s to deepen your relationship with who you are.

About the MARI® Assessment

The MARI® (Mandala Assessment Research Instrument) is a clinically validated assessment tool that uses creative art expression and symbolic imagery to access parts of your inner world that words alone may not reach. It offers a non-verbal pathway to self-understanding. No artistic skill is needed, only a willingness to explore.

Included With the Series

Your Companion Self-Discovery Journal

Designed to support reflection, deepen self-awareness, and help you integrate your discoveries between sessions and long after the Series ends.

Is This You?

The Self-Discovery path may be a good fit if you:

  • Have spent years caring for everyone else and are no longer sure what you need
  • Are coming out of a relationship that made you smaller, or still finding your footing after one
  • Are moving through a life transition, such as divorce, loss, children leaving home, or midlife, and do not recognize yourself in it
  • Look fine on the outside and feel disconnected on the inside
  • Have been in therapy before and sense something deeper has not been reached
  • Want focused, uninterrupted time for this work rather than working through it week by week
  • Are a clinician or helping professional seeking your own deep personal work

Eating Disorder Intensives

For when food and your body are where it shows

You know the pattern. You could probably explain it better than most people. The evening binge after a day of holding it together. The mirror that decides what kind of day it will be. The recovery that keeps stalling in the same place, no matter how well you understand why.

Understanding was never the missing piece. What drives binge eating and body image struggles lives underneath insight: in shame, in the body, in parts of you that learned food was the one reliable place to find relief or control. A 50-minute session often ends just as we reach it.

An intensive gives that work room. We can go into it, and still come back out, settled, before you leave.

1.5 to 2 Hours

Extended Sessions

Time to go into the shame, the grief, or the anger underneath the eating, and still leave grounded. Extended sessions can also be scheduled more often than weekly when you need more support than a weekly rhythm allows.

Learn More
3 to 4 Hours

Half-Day Intensives

A dedicated block of time, with breaks built in, for binge eating, body image, and patterns that have not shifted in weekly therapy. Somatic and expressive arts work help reach what talking alone has not.

Learn More

Is This You?

  • Binge eating or emotional eating that has not shifted, however much you understand about it
  • Body image that takes up more of your life than you want it to
  • Feeling stuck in recovery, or sliding back whenever stress rises
  • ADHD and trauma tangled up with how you eat
  • Being in weekly therapy, with me or someone else, and wanting more focused time

How We Begin

Eating disorder intensives start with care, not a booking. First, we meet for an initial assessment, so I understand your history and what is happening now. Next comes medical clearance from your physician, because an eating disorder can affect the body in ways that are not always visible. Then we decide together whether an intensive is the right fit, or whether weekly therapy or a different level of care would serve you better right now.

Intensives are outpatient care. If you need more support than outpatient work can safely offer, I will help you find the right next step. You can read more about how I work with eating disorders.
