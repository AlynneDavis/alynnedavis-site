Somatic Therapy & Breathwork in Charlotte, NC

Whether it’s trauma, burnout, a life transition, or a version of yourself you’re trying to find your way back to, your body has been holding on for a long time. Somatic therapy, breathwork, and the belief work underneath both help you finally put it down, not just understand it.

Your Body Is Talking. Somatic Work Teaches You to Listen.

Maybe you already understand yourself pretty well. You know your patterns, you can name your triggers, you’ve done the work of insight. And yet your chest still tightens before you’ve consciously registered why. Your breath still goes shallow in rooms that feel unsafe for reasons you can’t articulate. Understanding the pattern hasn’t been enough to change how your body responds to it.

That’s because regulation isn’t a thinking problem. It’s a body problem, and it needs a body-based solution. This is true whether the cause has a clear name, like trauma, or whether it’s harder to pin down, the low hum of burnout, a life transition that’s left you unsteady, a relationship pattern you keep repeating, or a quiet sense that you’ve lost touch with who you actually are underneath the roles you play.

I’m Alynne Davis, LCMHC, and in my work with women in Charlotte, NC and throughout North Carolina and South Carolina via telehealth, somatic therapy isn’t one isolated technique, and it isn’t only for people with a trauma history. It’s a combination: somatic awareness to notice what your body is holding, breathwork as a practical tool to shift your nervous system in real time, and mindset and belief work to change the thinking that keeps the old pattern running underneath it all, whatever brought you here.

How Somatics, Breathwork & Mindset Work Fit Together

These aren’t separate services or a fixed protocol I move through in order. They’re tools I bring in as needed, in session, depending on what’s actually in front of us that day. The reason they show up so often is simple: emotional regulation is at the root of most of what I treat. ADHD, binge eating, relationship patterns, stress, anxiety, and burnout all get harder to work with when your nervous system is dysregulated, and all get more workable once it isn’t. Somatic awareness, breathwork, and parts work are how we build that capacity. Mindset and belief work is what makes the shift stick.

Somatic Awareness

We slow down and notice what’s actually happening in your body, the tension, the bracing, the numbness, the flinch that doesn’t match the present moment. Not to analyze it, but to build a relationship with it. This is where you start learning your own nervous system’s language instead of just managing its symptoms.

Breathwork

Breathwork is one of the most direct tools I use for regulation, especially in the moment. Specific breathing patterns can shift you out of a fight-or-flight or shutdown state and back into a place where you can think clearly, feel more grounded, and stay present with whatever we’re working on. It also becomes something you can carry with you, a tool you learn in session and use in your actual life, whenever your nervous system needs help finding its way back.

Parts Work

Once your body has some room, once it’s not in pure survival mode, somatic awareness and breathwork also become doorways into parts work: noticing the different parts of you that developed to protect you. The part that overworks so no one can criticize you. The part that reaches for food the moment everything feels like too much. The part that shuts down in conflict rather than risk being too much. You don’t just talk about these parts, you can actually feel where they live in your body, which tends to build a faster, more honest relationship with them than insight alone.

Mindset & Belief Work

From that more regulated, more connected place, we go after what’s underneath the pattern: the beliefs that formed to keep you safe a long time ago. “I have to earn rest.” “I’m not safe unless I’m in control.” “My needs are too much.” Regulating your body and examining what you believe are not two separate projects. They work together, and real change tends to happen at the intersection of both.

Embodying Who You’re Becoming

The point of all of this isn’t just to feel calmer in the moment. It’s to actually become, to embody, the version of yourself you’re trying to grow into: steadier, more yourself, less run by old protective parts. That kind of change doesn’t happen in your head alone. Your body has to be part of how you get there, because it’s where the old patterns live, and it’s where the new ones actually take hold.

You Might Be Looking for Somatic Therapy If…

  • You understand your patterns intellectually but your body still reacts like nothing has changed
  • You feel anxious, tense, or on edge without always being able to point to why
  • You’re exhausted from a nervous system that won’t settle, even when things are objectively okay

  • You’re in the middle of a life transition, a move, a breakup, a career change, becoming a parent, a shift in identity, and it has left you feeling unsteady
  • You keep ending up in the same relationship pattern and want to understand what your body is doing that your mind hasn’t caught yet
  • You’re asking bigger questions about who you are, what you want, and whether the life you’ve built actually fits
  • You want practical tools, like breathwork, you can actually use, not just insight
  • You feel disconnected from your body, or like you live “from the neck up”
  • You suspect there are beliefs underneath your stress that talk therapy alone hasn’t shifted
  • You have ADHD and want regulation tools that actually work with your nervous system, not more willpower advice
  • You binge eat, or turn to food when things feel like too much, and want to understand what your body is doing in that moment
  • You’re managing trauma, chronic stress, or disordered eating and want an approach that works with your body, not just your thoughts

Somatic therapy isn’t only for people with a clear trauma history. Emotional regulation is at the root of most of what brings people to therapy in the first place, ADHD, binge eating, relationship patterns, anxiety, and burnout all get harder when your nervous system is dysregulated, and more workable once it isn’t, even when there’s no single event to point to. You don’t need prior experience with breathwork, meditation, or body-based practices of any kind. We start wherever you are, including if “I don’t really feel much in my body” is exactly where that is.

What a Session Looks Like

Check-In, In the Body

We start by checking in with what’s happening in your body right now, not just your thoughts, your breath, your posture, where you’re holding tension. This isn’t small talk. It tells us where to start.

Breathwork & Regulation, As Needed

If your nervous system is activated or shut down, we’ll use breathwork or another regulation tool first. It’s hard to do meaningful work from a dysregulated state, so this comes before anything else when it’s needed.

Tracking Sensation & What’s Underneath

From a more regulated place, we track what your body is holding and follow it to what’s underneath, often a belief, a protective pattern, or a part of you that’s been working hard to keep you safe.

Integration

We close by naming what shifted and what you can carry with you, often a specific breath or grounding practice you can use on your own between sessions.

In Person & Telehealth

I practice in Charlotte, NC and offer telehealth throughout North Carolina and South Carolina. Breathwork and body-awareness practices are guided verbally, so they translate well to telehealth, no in-person touch is involved.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is somatic therapy?

Somatic therapy is a body-based approach to healing that works with physical sensation, breath, and nervous system responses, not just thoughts and talk. Instead of only analyzing why you feel a certain way, somatic therapy helps you notice what is happening in your body right now, and gently teaches your nervous system that it is safe to settle. In sessions with me, somatic awareness is combined with breathwork and mindset or belief work, so that regulating the body and shifting the thoughts underneath it happen together.

Do you use breathwork in sessions?

Yes. Breathwork is used as one tool among several, not as a standalone practice or a separate appointment type. It’s woven into sessions when it’s useful for helping you regulate in the moment and understand what your nervous system is doing. Specific breathing patterns can shift you out of fight-or-flight or shutdown states and back into a place where you can think clearly and stay present with what you’re working on.

How does somatic therapy combine with mindset and belief work?

Regulating your body and changing your thinking aren’t two separate projects, they work together. Once breathwork and somatic awareness bring your nervous system out of a stress response, it becomes possible to actually examine the beliefs that formed underneath that stress: “I have to earn rest,” “I’m not safe unless I’m in control,” “my needs are too much.” I integrate somatic and breathwork practices with mindset and belief-shift work so that change happens in the body and in the thoughts that keep the old pattern running.

Is somatic therapy only for trauma?

No. While somatic therapy is well known for trauma work, most of what it addresses is really nervous system dysregulation, and that shows up in far more than trauma alone. I work with clients navigating burnout and chronic overwhelm, major life transitions, relationship patterns that keep repeating, identity questions, and everyday anxiety, in addition to trauma. If your body feels tense, activated, or shut down more often than it feels settled, somatic therapy can help, regardless of whether there’s a clear trauma history behind it.

Does somatic therapy involve being touched?

Not necessarily. My somatic work is talk- and awareness-based, I guide your attention to body sensation, breath, and nervous system states through questions and gentle prompts, not physical touch. You are always in control of what feels comfortable.

What if I can’t feel anything in my body?

That’s very common, especially if you have a history of trauma, chronic stress, or disordered eating. Numbness and disconnection are protective responses, not failures. Somatic work starts wherever you are, even if that starting point is “I don’t feel much of anything.” Breathwork and gentle body awareness practices build that connection back gradually, at a pace your nervous system can tolerate.

Is somatic therapy available via telehealth in North Carolina?

Yes. I offer somatic therapy and breathwork-integrated sessions in person in Charlotte, NC and via telehealth throughout North Carolina and South Carolina. Breathwork and body-awareness practices translate well to telehealth since they’re guided verbally and don’t require in-person touch.
