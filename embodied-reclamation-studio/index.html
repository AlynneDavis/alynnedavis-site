Embodied Reclamation

Come Back to the Woman You Were Never Meant to Lose

The body of work beneath everything I do, in therapy, in intensives, and in coaching.

There are seasons of life when we forget who we are.

Trauma. Shame. Survival. Relationships. Expectations. Old stories. The quiet ways we learn to leave ourselves behind in order to cope, belong, be loved, or simply make it through.

But forgetting yourself does not have to be the end of your story.

You can remember who you are.

You can return to yourself.

You can reclaim your life.

This is the work of Embodied Reclamation: learning to stop abandoning yourself and begin living from self-trust, self-compassion, creativity, choice, and a deeper relationship with the woman you already are.

Remember → Return → Reclaim

Begin Your Reclamation

You Do Not Need to Become Someone Else

You may have spent years believing that healing meant fixing yourself.

Be more disciplined.

Be more confident.

Be easier to love.

Take up less space.

Control your body.

Stop feeling so much.

Try harder.

But what if your next chapter is not about becoming a better version of yourself?

What if it is about coming back to the parts of you that were buried beneath shame, survival, fear, and the stories you learned to believe?

The woman underneath all of that is still there.

Your voice.

Your body.

Your creativity.

Your desires.

Your intuition.

Your courage.

Your capacity to choose.

This work is about finding your way back to her.

The Remember → Return → Reclaim Method

I created this work from a path I have lived myself.

There were times in my own life when trauma, shame, relationships, and beliefs about who I was caused me to lose contact with myself.

My way back was not one single breakthrough. It was a return, again and again, through art, movement, music, writing, nature, adventure, spirituality, self-compassion, and the courageous choices that slowly taught me I could trust myself.

Over time, returning became reclaiming.

And reclaiming became a way of living.

Today, I help women walk that path for themselves.

One

Remember

Remember the woman beneath the survival.

Before you can reclaim your life, you begin by noticing where you disappeared from it.

The beliefs you inherited.

The shame you carried.

The roles you learned to perform.

The coping strategies that once protected you.

The relationships or experiences that taught you to distrust yourself.

Remembering is not about romanticizing who you used to be. It is about reconnecting with the deeper truth of who you are beneath everything you learned you had to become.

You begin to ask:

Who am I when I am not performing?

What do I actually want?

What parts of myself have I judged, silenced, hidden, or abandoned?

What if I meet those parts with compassion instead?

Two

Return

Return to yourself through experience.

Insight matters.

But sometimes you cannot think your way back to yourself.

You have to experience your way back.

Through movement. Through creativity. Through music. Through writing. Through nature. Through adventure. Through spirituality. Through your body. Through trying something new. Through allowing pleasure, curiosity, expression, and aliveness to return.

Nature can become part of that return. The quiet of the trees. The steadiness of the earth beneath your feet. The rhythm of water. The spaciousness of the sky. The reminder that life moves in seasons.

Nature can help you soften enough to hear yourself again. It can remind you that growth does not always happen through force. Sometimes it happens through stillness. Through noticing. Through breathing. Through being present long enough to remember that you belong here too.

This is where you begin practicing a different relationship with yourself.

You learn to listen instead of override.

To feel instead of immediately numb.

To offer yourself compassion instead of shame.

To become curious about your body instead of treating it like a problem to solve.

To make choices that reflect who you are becoming.

Returning is the practice of choosing yourself again.

And again.

And again.

Three

Reclaim

Reclaim the life that becomes possible when you stop leaving yourself behind.

Reclamation is where insight becomes embodiment.

You do not only understand that you are worthy. You begin living like you believe it.

You stop organizing your life around shame. You stop needing food, perfectionism, people-pleasing, or self-abandonment to carry every difficult emotion.

You begin making peace with your body.

You learn to trust your own voice.

You leave what harms you.

You move through transitions you once believed were impossible.

You create.

You choose.

You take up space in your own life.

You become the woman who knows:

I can stand beside myself.

I can trust myself.

I can choose my life.

That is Embodied Reclamation.

This Work Is for the Woman Who Is Tired of Leaving Herself

Maybe from the outside, you look capable. You keep going. You take care of what needs to be taken care of. You have survived things other people may never fully understand.

And yet somewhere along the way, you lost contact with yourself.

Maybe you have used food to soothe what felt impossible to hold.

Maybe you have spent years at war with your body.

Maybe shame has shaped the way you see yourself.

Maybe you have stayed too long in relationships that asked you to disappear.

Maybe you have left a harmful relationship, or know something in your life must change, and now you are standing in the unfamiliar territory of becoming someone you have never had permission to be.

Maybe you have spent so much time being who everyone else needed that you no longer know what you need.

You do not need more shame.

You do not need another person telling you how you should be living.

You need a place where you can begin hearing yourself again.

What Becomes Possible When You Stop Abandoning Yourself

Reclamation does not mean becoming fearless or never struggling again. It means changing who stands beside you when life becomes difficult.

Instead of becoming your own critic, you learn to become your own ally. Instead of treating your body like the enemy, you begin listening to it. Instead of organizing your choices around shame, you begin choosing from self-respect. Instead of asking everyone else who you should be, you begin trusting the voice within you.

This work can support you in:

  • loosening the grip of shame and learning deeper self-compassion
  • changing your relationship with food and coping
  • moving toward greater acceptance and connection with your body
  • rebuilding self-trust after trauma or harmful relationships
  • navigating divorce, reinvention, and major life transitions
  • reconnecting with creativity, nature, pleasure, spirituality, and expression
  • making choices that reflect the life you actually want
  • becoming increasingly able to advocate for yourself, choose yourself, and trust yourself

The goal is not perfection.

The goal is belonging to yourself again.

I Know This Work Because I Have Lived It

I am not standing outside this journey telling you what reclamation should look like.

I have forgotten myself.

I have believed stories rooted in shame.

I have known what it is to disconnect from my body and use coping strategies to survive what I did not yet know how to hold.

I have known relationships and experiences that required me to rediscover my own voice.

I have walked through endings, reinventions, and choices that once felt impossible.

And I have learned that returning to yourself is rarely one dramatic moment. It is built through hundreds of small acts of courage.

A compassionate thought.

A boundary.

A painting.

A walk outside.

A song.

A journal page.

A trail.

A new experience.

A quiet moment under an open sky.

A decision not to abandon yourself this time.

This is the work I now bring to other women through therapy, intensives, and coaching.

Different containers.

One body of work: Remember. Return. Reclaim.

You Are Allowed to Become Your Own Hero

There comes a point in reclamation when something changes.

You stop waiting for someone else to give you permission. You stop believing your life belongs to everyone except you.

You begin discovering that the person you needed beside you has been growing within you all along.

Becoming your own hero is not about doing everything alone. It is about knowing that you will no longer abandon yourself in order to be chosen by someone else.

It is being able to say:

I am here.

I am listening.

I believe you.

I will not leave you this time.

That changes everything.

Your Reclamation Can Begin Here

You do not have to know exactly who you are becoming. You do not need the whole plan. You only need enough willingness to begin turning toward yourself instead of away.
