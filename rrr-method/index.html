The RRR Method™

Remember. Return. Reclaim. Healing is not about fixing who you are. It is about understanding what shaped you, reconnecting with the parts of yourself you lost along the way, and reclaiming the freedom to live as who you actually are.

Schedule a Free Consultation

How It Fits Together

Embodied Reclamation is the philosophy. Healing is not repair. The self you are trying to become is the one you already were, before you learned otherwise.

The RRR Method™ is the pathway through it. Remember, Return, Reclaim.

Compassionate honesty is the principle that governs all three phases. It is how you begin telling yourself the truth without abandoning yourself in the process.

And four ideas run underneath the whole thing: conditioned agreements and conditioned hope, which are what you inherited, and embodied permission and embodied choice, which are what becomes available.

Compassionate Honesty

Compassionate honesty is how you begin telling yourself the truth without abandoning yourself in the process.

Everything here runs on that one thing.

You cannot change what you will not look at. And you cannot look at it if looking costs more shame than you can carry.

Honesty without compassion is just another critic, and you have had enough of those. Compassion without honesty is reassurance, which feels kind and changes nothing.

Holding both at once is the actual skill. It is also, for most of the women I work with, the thing nobody ever modelled.

At the Centre

The three phases are not steps you complete and leave behind. They overlap, and they keep overlapping, because what sits at the centre of all three is the same thing.

Your self. Not a better version you have to build. The one that was there before you learned what you had to be, and that has been there the whole time underneath everything you built to protect it.

Remember, Return and Reclaim are three different ways of getting back to that centre. You will move through all of them more than once.

Return is not the middle phase. It is the one that touches the centre, which is why the other two depend on it. You return in order to remember, because some of what shaped you will not come back through thinking. And you return in order to reclaim, because insight only becomes a life when your body has been part of the process.

You will be returning the whole way through.

Remember

Understanding who you became

Before you can live more honestly, you have to get curious about how you learned to be who you are.

It starts early. You work out what makes you lovable, what keeps you safe, how relationships function, and what you have to do to belong. That understanding gets built out of your family, your attachments, what happened to you, what was expected of you, and what you were praised or punished for.

Over time it stops feeling like something you learned and starts feeling like something you are.

Here is the sequence, and it is the heart of this work. A belief forms. The belief hardens into an identity. The identity generates an internal agreement about who you have to be and what you have to tolerate. And the agreement starts running your behaviour, which produces exactly the experiences that prove the belief was right.

I am too much, so you shrink. You shrink, so nobody sees you. Nobody sees you, so you must be too much.

Remember is where you start taking that apart.

Conditioned agreements. These are terms you accepted without ever being asked. I will be easy. I will not need much. I will be the one who copes. Nobody wrote them down and you never agreed out loud, but you have kept to them for years, and breaking one still feels enormous.

Conditioned hope. This is the cruellest part of the structure, because it does not feel like a trap. It feels like optimism.

When I lose the weight. When I finally get it right. When they see how hard I am trying. Then.

Hope, but hope whose terms were set by the same belief that is hurting you. It keeps you inside the agreement rather than freeing you from it, and it is very hard to let go of, because giving it up looks like giving up.

And here is why that is not a bleak piece of work. None of it destroyed you. The self that was there before any of it was formed is still there, underneath the adaptations and the agreements and the roles. It was never broken. It was covered over, by things that at the time you genuinely needed.

So this is not about blaming your parents, rejecting the parts of you that adapted, or hunting for what is wrong with you. Those adaptations worked. They got you here. It is about telling the difference between who you are and who you learned you had to be.

Awareness is where possibility starts.

Return

Coming back to yourself

Understanding your patterns is a beginning. It is rarely enough.

You can see the whole thing clearly, name it, explain it to a friend, and still watch yourself do it again on Thursday. That is not failure. It is what happens when something lives in your body and you keep addressing it in your head.

Return moves from understanding into experience. Through your body, your emotions, your creativity, your relationships, and the ordinary things that hold a life together.

That means somatic awareness, expressive arts, movement, play, and self-compassion. It also means the unglamorous parts. Food. Sleep. Rest. Pleasure. Who you spend your time with.

Not as another set of rules to follow perfectly. You have had enough of those. The question is not what you should be doing. It is what actually nourishes you, and what you can sustain.

Creativity matters here more than people expect. Making something without having to be good at it is one of the few ways left to be in your own experience without performing. That is often where the parts of you that got buried under responsibility start turning up again.

Embodied permission. There is a difference between knowing you are allowed to rest and being able to actually rest. Between agreeing that your needs matter and feeling, in your body, that it is safe to have one. The first is a thought. The second is permission your system believes, and it is only ever built through experience.

Return is not about becoming perfectly regulated. It is about being on better terms with yourself.

Reclaim

Living as who you are

As you understand what shaped you and come back into relationship with yourself, you get choices you did not have before.

Reclaim is where that becomes a life.

You start questioning the agreements that no longer fit. You set boundaries and keep them. You ask for more reciprocal relationships. You say what you think. You make decisions that account for your own needs rather than organising around everyone else’s.

Sometimes it means picking up something you put down years ago. Sometimes it means changing a pattern in a relationship, or finally resting without earning it first.

Underneath all of it is one shift. Your worth stops being something you have to keep earning through achievement, appearance, productivity, caretaking, or being easy to be around.

Reclaim does not mean you stop feeling fear or self-doubt, or that the old patterns never show up again. They will. It means you have room to respond differently when they do.

Embodied choice. A choice made from a settled, present body is a different thing from a reaction fired off by an old agreement. From the outside both look like a decision. Only one of them is yours.

You are not becoming someone new. You are giving yourself permission to be who you already are.

A Pathway, Not a Finish Line

These are not three steps in order. They do not work that way in practice.

You cannot remember from your head alone. Some of what shaped you is not stored as a memory you can retrieve by thinking harder about it. You come back into your body, and something surfaces that no amount of analysis was ever going to produce. So returning is not what happens after remembering. It is often how remembering happens at all.

And reclaiming does not finish. Every stage of a life asks something new of you. A relationship ends. A parent dies. Your body changes. Your children leave. Something you built stops fitting. Each time, old agreements surface again in new clothes, and you go round once more, from a different place and with more of yourself available than you had last time.

That is not failing to complete the work. That is the work.

Meaningful change does not come from becoming more worthy. It comes from the awareness, compassion and nerve it takes to honour who you already are.

You remember what shaped you. You return to yourself. And you reclaim the freedom to live, love and create without abandoning yourself to do it.
