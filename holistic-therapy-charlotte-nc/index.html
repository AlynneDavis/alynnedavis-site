Holistic Therapy for Women in Charlotte, NC

Whole-person therapy that looks at your life, not just your symptoms. In person in Charlotte, and online across North Carolina and South Carolina.

You Are More Than a Diagnosis

You have probably been asked to describe your symptoms, rate your mood, and check boxes on an intake form. Useful, sometimes. But none of it captures that you have not slept properly in two years, that you eat standing up over the sink, that you stopped seeing your friends, or that you no longer feel connected to anything larger than your to-do list.

Holistic therapy means we look at all of it. Not because everything is your responsibility to fix, but because your mental health does not live in isolation from the rest of your life. It never has.

What We Look At Together

These are not separate from your mental health. They are your mental health.

Nourishment

How you eat, whether you eat, and what food has come to mean to you. Blood sugar, restriction, and years of diet culture all shape mood, focus, and how safe your body feels.

Sleep

Almost nothing recovers without it. We look at what is actually keeping you awake, which is often not what you would guess.

Movement

Not exercise as punishment or a way to earn food. Movement as a way to discharge stress and return to a body that has felt like enemy territory.

Relationships

Who you can be honest with, who you shrink around, and how much you are carrying for other people. Connection regulates the nervous system more powerfully than almost anything else.

Spirituality & Meaning

Whatever that word means to you, or does not. A sense of something larger, of purpose, of being part of something. Its absence is felt, even when it goes unnamed.

Self-Care & Environment

Not bubble baths. Rest you do not have to earn, boundaries you can hold, and whether the space you live in soothes you or drains you.

How This Actually Works

Holistic does not mean unstructured, and it does not mean I will hand you a supplement list. I am a Licensed Clinical Mental Health Counselor. The clinical work is real: trauma therapy, eating disorder treatment, ADHD support, and somatic and nervous system work.

What makes it holistic is the lens. When you tell me you have been snapping at your kids, we do not only examine the thought underneath it. We also look at whether you have eaten, whether you have slept, whether you have had a full conversation with another adult this week, and what your body has been bracing against.

Sometimes the intervention is a boundary. Sometimes it is grief. Sometimes it is protein and an earlier bedtime. Most often it is several of these at once, which is exactly why treating one in isolation has not worked.

I also work with creative expression, because some of what you are carrying has no words attached to it yet. You do not need to be artistic for this to help.

This May Be a Fit If…

  • You have done therapy before and it helped, but something still felt unaddressed.
  • You have been handed a diagnosis that explained a piece of it, but not the whole of it.
  • You are exhausted in a way that sleep alone does not touch.
  • Your relationship with food, your body, or rest is tangled up with how you feel about yourself.
  • You want to understand the whole picture, not just manage symptoms.
  • You are in a season of change and are not sure who you are on the other side of it.

I offer holistic therapy in person in Charlotte, NC and via telehealth throughout North Carolina and South Carolina, including Raleigh, Asheville, Durham, Wilmington, Columbia SC, and Greenville SC.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is holistic therapy?

Holistic therapy treats you as a whole person rather than a set of symptoms. Alongside traditional talk therapy, it considers how sleep, nourishment, movement, relationships, spirituality, self-care, and your environment affect your mental health, because all of them do.

Is holistic therapy still real therapy?

Yes. I am a Licensed Clinical Mental Health Counselor practicing evidence-based therapy. Holistic describes how wide the lens is, not a departure from clinical care.

Will you tell me what to eat or give me supplements?

No. I am not a dietitian or a physician, and I do not prescribe supplements or meal plans. We explore your relationship with food and nourishment therapeutically, and I refer to trusted medical and nutrition colleagues when that is what you need.

Do I have to be spiritual or religious?

Not at all. Spirituality means different things to different women, and for some it means nothing. We follow your definition, or leave it out entirely.

How is this different from the therapy I have tried before?

Most therapy focuses on thoughts and behaviors, which matters. Holistic therapy adds the rest of the context: your body, your nervous system, your daily rhythms, and your relationships. Many women find that the piece that was missing lived in that context.

Do you offer holistic therapy online?

Yes. I offer in-person sessions in Charlotte, NC and secure telehealth throughout North Carolina and South Carolina.
