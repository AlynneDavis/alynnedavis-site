Healing One Memory Without Reliving It

You may have been told that healing means going back through the worst of it, in detail, again and again. It doesn’t have to. There is a gentler way to work with a memory that keeps running your body.

You Don’t Have to Dig Up the Past

Predictive Processing Flash, formerly known as the Four Blinks version of Flash, is a trauma reprocessing approach developed by therapist Thomas Zimmerman. It works on one memory at a time, with the lowest activation possible, so you can process it without being retraumatized.

The memory doesn’t have to be old. A recent moment when you were activated is a perfectly good place to start. And you don’t have to tell me everything that happened.

What It’s Like

We always begin with resourcing, building a sense of steadiness and choosing a calm scene that feels good to you. Then we practice on a memory that isn’t very activating, so you understand exactly what we’ll be doing before we go anywhere near something harder.

When we work on a memory, we approach only a sliver of it at a time, then close it and turn your attention to the calm scene. Looking at the calm scene during the processing keeps activation low, so there is less of it, if any. The idea is that your nervous system expects something bad to follow the memory, and the calm scene doesn’t match that expectation. That mismatch may help your system update what it predicts.

We repeat this sliver by sliver, then go back over the whole memory to check that nothing is still activating. I don’t ask you to rate your distress on a scale, because being asked to measure it can make you more activated just by thinking about it.

Who It Can Be a Good Fit For

  • You’re afraid of retelling your story, and want to work on it without narrating it
  • A recent moment activated you, and you want to work with that, not only the distant past
  • You want a slower, gentler pace than trauma work you may have tried before

It sits alongside the parts work, expressive arts, and body-based approaches I already use. We decide together whether it fits and when, since pacing matters, especially with complex trauma. Read more about how I work with trauma.

About This Approach

I trained directly with Thomas Zimmerman after Hurricane Katrina, to support survivors of acute trauma. His work continues to be researched and developed, including for parts work and complex PTSD. You can learn more at fourblinks.com.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Predictive Processing Flash?

It is a trauma reprocessing approach developed by Thomas Zimmerman, formerly known as the Four Blinks version of Flash. It works on one memory at a time with the lowest activation possible, so you can process it without being retraumatized.

Do I have to tell you what happened?

No. The memory is only opened briefly, a sliver at a time, and you don’t need to narrate it in detail. It also doesn’t have to be old. A recent moment when you were activated is a good place to start.

Is this the same as the Flash Technique?

It comes out of the Flash family of approaches, but Thomas Zimmerman describes it as conceptually and practically different from the original version taught by Dr. Phil Manfield.

Is it right for complex trauma?

Fit and pacing matter, especially with complex trauma. We talk together about whether it makes sense for you and when. Zimmerman continues to develop the approach, including for parts work and C-PTSD.

Where did you train in this approach?

I trained directly with Thomas Zimmerman after Hurricane Katrina to support survivors of acute trauma.
